Welcome, little swimmer!

Every swimmer learns differently. Take your time, practise with a trusted adult or qualified instructor, and celebrate every little step.

There is no fixed lesson duration. One activity or lesson may take one session, two sessions, or more.

💧 Take it slowly  ·  practise together  ·  celebrate every step

👩‍🏫 For the young coach — and the first learner

Coaching a first learner? Open one lesson at a time. Show the picture, try one step together, tick it off, and celebrate. That is the whole lesson.

Learning for the first time? Follow the big numbers, 1 → 2 → 3 → 4. There is no rush — one step, one session, or more.

How to start swimming

Module 1: eight little steps, in order. Then Module 2 (Lessons 9–16) takes you to freestyle and backstroke. Tap a lesson below to open it.

🛟 Water safety comes first

An adult must actively supervise the child and stay within arm’s reach. Never leave a child alone in or near water. Floatation aids do not replace supervision.

Never force submersion or practise breath-holding competitions.

Lesson 2

Calm Breathing

Learn relaxed breathing and become more comfortable around water.

✓ Done ⌄

👀 Watch, then try — the movement steps

1

Breathe calmly, away from the water

Slow, relaxed breathing first — on dry land, with no water to worry about.

✋ Try this: calm breathing away from the water
2

Blow bubbles at the surface

With an instructor beside you, blow gently at the water surface — little bubbles, slow and calm.

✋ Try this: blow bubbles at the water surface
3

Face wet again, if needed

If it feels tricky, go back a step — getting the face wet is always there to practise again.

✋ Try this: return to getting the face wet
👩‍🏫 Coach tip
Calm breathing comes before the water. If breathing is calm, the rest feels easier.
⚠️ Watch out
Never force a child to put their face underwater or hold their breath. Keep all practice supervised.

🔍 Break it down — the breathing cycle

  • Step 1 — Breathe in: take a big, calm breath in through the mouth, away from the water first.
  • Step 2 — Blow out slowly: lower the mouth to the surface and blow the air out gently — no rushing.
  • Step 3 — Watch the bubbles: the air makes bubbles in the water, just like blowing soap bubbles.
  • Step 4 — Lift and repeat: lift your head, breathe in, and go again. If it feels tricky, return to getting the face wet.

Lesson 5

Kicking

Gentle leg kicks, holding the wall or a kickboard, with support.

✓ Done ⌄
🎯 Learning goal
Kick gently from the hips, with relaxed ankles and pointed toes, and keep kicking while you hold on.

👀 Watch, then try — the movement steps

Look at each picture together, then try that one step. Small, relaxed kicks beat big, fast ones.

1

Hold the wall

Hold the pool wall with both hands, body stretched out flat on the water, face down or looking down.

✋ Try this: hold the wall, body flat
2

Small kicks from the hips

Kick up and down, one leg at a time, from the hips — not just from the knees. Toes pointed, ankles loose and floppy like a fish tail.

✋ Try this: small kicks, hips first
3

Kick with a kickboard

Hold a kickboard with both hands, arms stretched out in front, and kick gently across a short distance, with an instructor beside you.

✋ Try this: kickboard kick, short and slow
4

Kick on your back

Float on your back like a starfish, then add the same small kicks, with an instructor supporting your head and body.

✋ Try this: back kick, with support
👩‍🏫 Coach tip
Kick from the hips, with slightly bent knees and relaxed ankles. If the feet splash high out of the water, slow down and kick smaller.
⚠️ Watch out
Never practise kicking alone. Hold the wall or a kickboard, and keep an adult or instructor within arm's reach.

🔍 Break it down — kicking, step by step

  • Step 1 — Hold on: hold the wall or a kickboard, arms stretched out, body flat on the water.
  • Step 2 — Kick from the hips: legs move up and down, one at a time. The hips start the kick, not the knees.
  • Step 3 — Loose ankles: ankles stay loose and floppy, toes pointed, so the feet push the water like fins.
  • Step 4 — Small splashes: feet just break the surface with little splashes. Big splashes mean slow down and kick smaller.

Lesson 6

Arms and Breathing Together

Put aeroplane arms and calm breathing together, one slow step at a time.

✓ Done ⌄
🎯 Learning goal
Move one arm at a time, keep kicking gently, and breathe out in the water, then in when you turn your head to the side.

👀 Watch, then try — the movement steps

Do one thing at a time first. Only put arms and breathing together when each part already feels easy.

1

One arm at a time

Reach one arm forward like an aeroplane, pull it back through the water, then reach again. The other arm stays stretched out in front.

✋ Try this: one arm, slow and smooth
2

Keep kicking while you pull

Keep the small, gentle kicks going from Lesson 5 while one arm pulls. Kick, pull, kick, pull — nice and slow.

✋ Try this: kick + one arm, together
3

Breathe to the side

As one arm pulls, turn your head gently to that side, take a quick breath in, then turn your face back down and breathe out slowly in the water.

✋ Try this: turn, breathe in, face down, blow out
4

Swap arms, keep going

Swap to the other arm and do the same again. Rest whenever you need to — stand up, hold the wall, breathe calmly.

✋ Try this: swap arms, rest when you need to
👩‍🏫 Coach tip
Count it out: “kick, kick, kick, pull, breathe.” Six gentle kicks, then one arm pull and a breath — that slow rhythm is the secret. If breathing feels hard, go back to blowing bubbles (Lesson 2) and try again.
⚠️ Watch out
Never lift your whole head up to breathe — that makes the legs sink. Turn your head to the side, keep one eye in the water, and breathe in quickly. Stop and stand up if you feel tired or worried.

🔍 Break it down — arms and breathing, step by step

  • Step 1 — Reach: one arm reaches forward, fingertips first, while the other arm stays stretched out.
  • Step 2 — Pull + kick: pull that arm back through the water, and keep the gentle kicks going the whole time.
  • Step 3 — Turn and breathe in: as the arm pulls, turn your head to the same side and take a quick breath in. Keep one eye in the water.
  • Step 4 — Face down, blow out, swap: turn your face back down, blow the air out slowly in the water, then swap arms and do it again.

Lesson 7

Swimming a Short Distance

Front paddle for 5 metres, with support — your first little swim.

✓ Done ⌄
🎯 Learning goal
Swim 5 metres on your front, using arms, kicks and breathing together, and stop safely at the wall or with your instructor.

👀 Watch, then try — the movement steps

Five metres is the goal — about the length of a small car. Start shorter, and build up one metre at a time.

1

Push off and glide

Push gently off the wall, arms stretched out in front, body flat like a torpedo. Glide for a moment before you start moving your arms.

✋ Try this: push, glide, then start
2

Front paddle, arms and kicks

Paddle with your arms, one at a time, and keep the gentle kicks going — just like Lesson 6, but now you keep going.

✋ Try this: paddle and kick, keep going
3

Breathe, then keep going

Turn your head to the side for a quick breath when you need one, then face down and keep swimming. Rest by standing up or floating whenever you need to.

✋ Try this: breathe, then keep going
4

Stop safely — 5 metres!

Reach the wall, or your instructor's hands, and stop. Hold on, stand up, and breathe calmly. You swam 5 metres — celebrate!

⭐ Try this: stop safely, then celebrate!
👩‍🏫 Coach tip
Build it up: 2 metres, then 3, then 5. Put a toy or a kickboard 5 metres away as a finish line — swimming towards something is much easier than swimming for no reason.
⚠️ Watch out
Only swim a short distance in shallow water, with an instructor or adult in the water beside you. If you feel tired, roll onto your back and float like a starfish, or stand up and hold the wall.

🔍 Break it down — your first 5 metres

  • Step 1 — Push and glide: push gently off the wall, arms out front, body flat. Glide before you start your arms.
  • Step 2 — Paddle and kick: arms paddle one at a time, legs keep the gentle kicks going.
  • Step 3 — Breathe and keep going: turn your head to the side for a breath when you need one, then face down and keep swimming.
  • Step 4 — Stop safely: reach the wall or your instructor, hold on, stand up, breathe calmly — and celebrate your 5 metres.

Lesson 8

Water Safety and What to Do Next

Pool rules, never swim alone, what to practise next, and when to join a class.

✓ Done ⌄
🎯 Learning goal
Know the pool rules by heart, always swim with an adult, and know what to practise next — and when to join a proper swimming class.

👀 Learn the rules — one by one

These rules keep you safe every time you swim. Say each one out loud with your adult, then tick it off when you remember it.

1

Never swim alone

Always swim with a buddy, and always with an adult watching. Accidents can happen quickly — having someone nearby can save a life.

✋ Try this: say it — “Never swim alone”
2

Stay within arm's reach

Stay close enough for your adult to touch you. That is the “touch rule” — if your adult cannot reach you, you are too far away.

✋ Try this: stay where your adult can reach you
3

Follow the pool rules

No running by the pool, no diving in shallow water, no pushing or rough play, and listen to the lifeguard and pool staff.

✋ Try this: walk, don't run, by the pool
4

Know how to get out

Always know where the steps or ladder are, and how to get out of the pool safely. Tell an adult before you get in the water.

✋ Try this: point to the pool steps before you swim
👩‍🏫 Coach tip
Good swimmers are safe swimmers. The best thing you can do next is keep practising Lessons 1–7, a little at a time, and ask your adult about joining a proper swimming class with a qualified instructor.
⚠️ Watch out
Swim lessons cannot “drown-proof” you. Nobody should swim alone — not even a good swimmer. One adult should always be watching the water, with no distractions.
🛟 If someone needs help
Call an adult or the lifeguard straight away. Throw something that floats to them — a ring, a board, a ball — and keep talking to them. Do not jump in after them. A grown-up who knows how to help should do the rescue.

🔍 Break it down — what to practise next

  • Keep practising: blow bubbles, starfish float, gentle kicks, arms and breathing, and your 5-metre swim — a little at a time, every time you go to the pool.
  • Try a longer swim: when 5 metres feels easy, try 10 metres — only with your instructor, and only in shallow water to start.
  • Try swimming on your back: back float, back kick, and back paddle — with your instructor beside you.
  • Join a class: ask your adult about a local swimming class for children aged 4–10, with a qualified instructor. A class gives you regular practice, new friends, and a teacher who can help you learn the next strokes.
  • Keep your cap ready: a swim cap that fits well keeps hair out of your eyes and helps you feel like a real swimmer.
  • Wear a lifejacket on a boat: on a boat, or near open water, wear a lifejacket that fits you well. A lifejacket is not a toy — it helps keep you afloat if you fall in.

Lesson 9

Freestyle Kick, Stronger and Longer

Kick from the hips with loose ankles — on your front and on your back.

✓ Done ⌄
🎯 Learning goal
Kick from the hips with loose, floppy ankles, and kick 10 metres with a kickboard — on your front and on your back.

👀 Watch, then try — the kicking steps

Start in shallow water with your adult beside you. Do one step at a time, and rest whenever you need to.

1

Kick from the hips

Hold the pool edge. Keep your legs long and kick from the top of your legs, not from your knees.

✋ Try this: kick at the wall for 10 kicks
2

Loose, floppy ankles

Let your feet wobble like fish tails. Toes point back. Small, fast kicks make little splashes.

✋ Try this: wiggle your feet like a fish tail
3

Kickboard kick 10 m

Hold the sides of the kickboard, arms long, and kick across the shallow end. Blow bubbles if your face is in.

✋ Try this: kick 10 m with a board
4

Kick on your back

Lie on your back with the board hugged on your tummy. Ears in the water, tummy up, small kicks.

✋ Try this: kick on your back for 5 m, then 10 m
👩‍🏫 Coach tip
Say “long legs, floppy feet”. Count kicks out loud — 1, 2, 3, 4 — and stop to rest and breathe after every short kick. Rest, breathe, kick again.
⚠️ Watch out
Big bicycle kicks with bent knees push you backwards, not forwards. Stay in shallow water where you can stand, and keep your adult within arm’s reach. Never swim alone.
🏁 After this lesson
Kicks 10 m with a kickboard, front and back.

🔍 Break it down — kicking, step by step

  • Step 1 — At the wall: hold the edge, legs long, 10 small kicks, then rest.
  • Step 2 — Board on the front: kick 5 m, rest, then 10 m.
  • Step 3 — Board on the back: hug the board, ears in, tummy up, kick 5 m, then 10 m.
  • Step 4 — Rest and repeat: rest, breathe, kick again. Two or three tries is plenty.

Lesson 10

Freestyle Arms, One at a Time

One arm pulls while the other rests — keep the legs kicking.

✓ Done ⌄
🎯 Learning goal
Pull with one arm while the other arm rests in front, keep kicking, swap arms slowly, then swim arms and kick together for 5 metres.

👀 Watch, then try — the arm steps

Practise the arm on land first, then in shallow water with your adult beside you.

1

One arm pulls, one rests

Stand tall. One arm stays up in front. The other arm reaches, pulls down past your leg, and comes back over the top.

✋ Try this: 5 slow pulls on land with each arm
2

Keep kicking

Hold a kickboard with one hand. Kick with floppy feet while the free arm makes slow pulls.

✋ Try this: kick and pull with one arm for 5 m
3

Swap arms slowly

Change hands on the board. Now the other arm pulls. Slow and long is better than fast.

✋ Try this: swap and pull with the other arm
4

Arms and kick 5 m

No board. Push gently from the wall, kick, and pull one arm, then the other — for 5 metres.

✋ Try this: 5 m arms and kick, then stand up
👩‍🏫 Coach tip
Use the words “reach, pull, push, over the top”. Watch for long arms in front. If the legs stop when the arm pulls, go back to the board for a while.
⚠️ Watch out
Arms that slap the water or cross in front of your nose make you wiggle. Keep each hand in line with its own shoulder. Stand up and breathe whenever you need to — never force your face under the water.

🔍 Break it down — one arm at a time

  • Step 1 — On land: stand and pull one arm 5 times, then the other arm.
  • Step 2 — With a board: one hand on the board, the other arm pulls, legs kicking.
  • Step 3 — Swap: change hands on the board and pull with the other arm.
  • Step 4 — No board: push off gently, kick, pull one arm then the other, 5 m.

Lesson 11

Freestyle Breathing to the Side

Turn your head to the side to breathe — no lifting your head up.

✓ Done ⌄
🎯 Learning goal
Turn your head to the side for a breath, then put your face back in and blow bubbles — first on land, then at the wall, then while you swim.

👀 Watch, then try — the breathing steps

Go slowly. Breathing is the hardest part, so practise it many times at the wall before you swim.

1

Turn your head on land

Stand and bend forward a little. Look down, then turn your head to the side so one ear is on your shoulder. Breathe in, turn back, blow out.

✋ Try this: 5 head turns on land
2

Side breath at the wall

Hold the wall with one hand. Face in, blow bubbles. Turn your head to the side, breathe in, face back in.

✋ Try this: 5 side breaths at the wall
3

One-arm pull + side breath

Holding a board with one hand, pull the other arm. As that arm pulls back, turn your head to the same side and breathe.

✋ Try this: pull and breathe 3 times
4

Two pulls, breathe, two pulls

Swim slowly: pull, pull, turn and breathe, then pull, pull again. Stop and stand whenever you need to.

✋ Try this: two pulls, breathe, two pulls
👩‍🏫 Coach tip
Say “one goggle in, one goggle out”. The head turns like a door — it does not lift up. Blow all the bubbles out under the water, so the breath in is quick and easy.
⚠️ Watch out
Lifting the head straight up makes the legs sink. Never force the face under the water — if a child is not ready, go back to blowing bubbles from Lesson 2. Stay where you can stand, with an adult within arm’s reach.

🔍 Break it down — side breathing, step by step

  • Step 1 — On land: bend forward, turn your head to the side, breathe in, turn back, blow out.
  • Step 2 — At the wall: one hand on the wall, face in, bubbles, turn, breathe.
  • Step 3 — With a board: pull one arm and turn to the same side to breathe.
  • Step 4 — Swim it: pull, pull, breathe; pull, pull, breathe — then stand and rest.
