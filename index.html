Welcome, little swimmer!

Every swimmer learns differently. Take your time, practise with a trusted adult or qualified instructor, and celebrate every little step.

There is no fixed lesson duration. One activity or lesson may take one session, two sessions, or more.

💧 Take it slowly  ·  practise together  ·  celebrate every step

👩‍🏫 For the young coach — and the first learner

Coaching a first learner? Open one lesson at a time. Show the picture, try one step together, tick it off, and celebrate. That is the whole lesson.

Learning for the first time? Follow the big numbers, 1 → 2 → 3 → 4. There is no rush — one step, one session, or more.

How to start swimming

Four little steps, in order. Tap a lesson below to open it.

🛟 Water safety comes first

An adult must actively supervise the child and stay within arm’s reach. Never leave a child alone in or near water. Floatation aids do not replace supervision.

Never force submersion or practise breath-holding competitions.

Lesson 2

Calm Breathing

Learn relaxed breathing and become more comfortable around water.

✓ Done ⌄

👀 Watch, then try — the movement steps

1

Breathe calmly, away from the water

Slow, relaxed breathing first — on dry land, with no water to worry about.

✋ Try this: calm breathing away from the water
2

Blow bubbles at the surface

With an instructor beside you, blow gently at the water surface — little bubbles, slow and calm.

✋ Try this: blow bubbles at the water surface
3

Face wet again, if needed

If it feels tricky, go back a step — getting the face wet is always there to practise again.

✋ Try this: return to getting the face wet
👩‍🏫 Coach tip
Calm breathing comes before the water. If breathing is calm, the rest feels easier.
⚠️ Watch out
Never force a child to put their face underwater or hold their breath. Keep all practice supervised.

🔍 Break it down — the breathing cycle

  • Step 1 — Breathe in: take a big, calm breath in through the mouth, away from the water first.
  • Step 2 — Blow out slowly: lower the mouth to the surface and blow the air out gently — no rushing.
  • Step 3 — Watch the bubbles: the air makes bubbles in the water, just like blowing soap bubbles.
  • Step 4 — Lift and repeat: lift your head, breathe in, and go again. If it feels tricky, return to getting the face wet.

🎥 Watch and learn — Lesson 2

Teaching breath control: Blowing bubbles in swim lessons — My Swim Guide, YouTube (free to watch).

What to watch for: the teacher takes a big, exaggerated breath in first, then blows out as the head lowers to the water — so the child can see the bubbles form. Try the same idea at home first with soap bubbles, then at the pool.

Watch together first, then try one slow breath at the water surface — with an instructor beside you.

Lesson 3

Floating and Kicking

Build comfort with supported floating and gentle leg movements.

✓ Done ⌄

👀 Watch, then try — the movement steps

1

Starfish float, relaxed

A relaxed starfish position, with instructor support — arms and legs soft, body calm.

✋ Try this: starfish float with support
2

Gentle kicks at the edge

Hold the pool edge and practise small, relaxed kicks — little splashes, not big ones.

✋ Try this: gentle kicks holding the pool edge
3

Repeat until it feels easy

Repeat until the child feels comfortable — and the instructor agrees it is right to move on.

✋ Try this: repeat, then check with your instructor
👩‍🏫 Coach tip
Small, relaxed kicks beat big, fast ones. Comfort first — progress follows.
⚠️ Watch out
Floating and kicking practice must be closely supervised. Do not practise unsupported floating alone.

🔍 Break it down — floating, step by step

  • Front starfish: instructor supports your body and head → relax arms and legs → spread out wide.
  • Back starfish: same again on your back — relax, arms and legs out, instructor supporting your body and head.
  • Ball float: with instructor support, gently curl into a ball shape. Do not hold your breath for a challenge.

Arms + gentle kicking

Put arms and gentle kicking together — only when ready. No rush.

First strokes — celebrate!

Your first strokes! Celebrate the effort, and keep learning with an instructor.

⭐ Try this: celebrate, then keep practising
👩‍🏫 Coach tip
Arms first, then add the kick — and only when the learner says they are ready.

🔍 Break it down — your first strokes

  • Step 1 — Arms: practise simple arm movements with guidance, one at a time, nice and slow.
  • Step 2 — Arms + kick: combine arm movements and gentle kicking only when ready.
  • Step 3 — Breathe: keep the calm breathing from Lesson 2 going while you move.
  • Step 4 — Celebrate: celebrate the effort, and continue learning with an instructor.
