Welcome, little swimmer! 🐬

Every swimmer learns differently. Take your time, practise with a trusted adult or qualified instructor, and celebrate every little step.

There is no fixed lesson duration. One activity or lesson may take one session, two sessions, or more.

💧
LESSON 1

Water Confidence

Become comfortable with water, practise gentle movements, and begin exploring floating with support.

Learn at your own pace
🎯 Learning goal
Feel more comfortable in the water, get the face wet when ready, blow bubbles, and try simple movements with support.

Part A: Getting comfortable

Try these activities gently. Stop, take a break, or repeat an activity whenever needed.

Part B: Moving and floating

These activities require close adult support. A qualified swimming instructor should guide floating practice.

💚 Parent tip
Praise effort, not speed. If a child feels worried, return to an activity they already enjoy and try again another day.
🛟 Water safety comes first
An adult must actively supervise the child and stay within arm’s reach. Never leave a child alone in or near water. Floatation aids do not replace supervision. Never force submersion or practise breath-holding competitions.

🌬️
LESSON 2

Calm Breathing

Learn relaxed breathing and become more comfortable around water.

  • Practise calm breathing away from the water first.
  • Blow bubbles at the water surface with an instructor.
  • Return to getting the face wet if needed.
Never force a child to put their face underwater or hold their breath. Keep all practice supervised.
⭐
LESSON 3

Floating and Kicking

Build comfort with supported floating and gentle leg movements.

  • Practise a relaxed starfish position with instructor support.
  • Try gentle kicks while holding the pool edge.
  • Repeat until the child feels comfortable and the instructor agrees it is appropriate to progress.
Floating and kicking practice must be closely supervised. Do not practise unsupported floating alone.
🐬
LESSON 4

First Swimming Strokes

Start basic arm and leg coordination with a qualified instructor.

  • Practise simple arm movements with guidance.
  • Combine arm movements and gentle kicking only when ready.
  • Celebrate effort and continue learning with an instructor.
This app is a practice companion, not a substitute for lessons from a qualified swimming instructor.
